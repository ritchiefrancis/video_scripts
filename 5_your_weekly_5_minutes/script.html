Your Weekly 5 Minutes

Target 1:00–1:30 VO ≈ 230 words The habit video British English

The brief: the habit video — snap a receipt, log a job payment, log your mileage, and the quarterly update fills itself in. This is the video that makes someone self-sufficient: the message is little-and-often, not software features. The beats play out across one ordinary working week — Tuesday's materials run, Thursday's payday, Friday's drive — so it feels like a routine someone copies, not a feature tour; a small on-screen day caption on beats 2–4 is enough to carry the thread. Keep it warm and unhurried. The audience is non-tax-savvy; no jargon.

Merge option: the catalogue offers #5 and #11 (Receipts) as one combined video — if we go that way, beat 2 below expands into the full receipts walkthrough and the target becomes ~2:00. This script stands alone as written.

0:00–0:12 The hook — little and often 27 words · ~12s

On screen

Open on the problem, not the product — the January shoebox of crumpled receipts, or a late-night laptop and a calculator. Your creative call. Cut to a phone propped next to a mug of tea: the five-minute version of the same job. (Prefer the mug over the van-dashboard variant — the finished Video 1 already opens on a van scene.)

Voiceover

Nobody wants to spend January drowning in a shoebox of receipts. So FileThat asks for something much smaller: five minutes, once a week. Here's one ordinary week.

0:12–0:36 Tuesday — snap a receipt 55 words · ~24s

On screen

Real-world to product in one motion (I'll record the product shots):
  • Phone camera over a builders' merchant receipt → photo taken
  • Receipts page → upload the photo → brief processing moment
  • The details appear, already read from the receipt → convert to a digital record
  • Hold on the confirmation: it's now a digital record, receipt image attached — caption: "Digital records feed your VAT & Self Assessment submissions"

Voiceover

Tuesday, and you're buying materials. Snap the receipt with your phone and upload it. FileThat reads the details off it for you — you just check them and convert it. That's the receipt saved as a digital record with the photo as proof — and it's those digital records that feed your VAT and Self Assessment submissions.

0:36–1:02 Thursday — log a job payment 56 words · ~24s

On screen

Digital Records page → Add a transaction → date, description (e.g. "Kitchen refit — final payment"), amount, income category → save → it appears in the transaction list alongside Tuesday's receipt. On the accountant line, caption: "Already have an accountant? The separate Income & Expenditure ledger keeps standalone records you can export and send over — no HMRC submissions needed."

Voiceover

Thursday: the kitchen job pays out. Add it to your digital records — the date, what it was, how much — and it's stored alongside your receipts, ready for your quarterly update. Got an accountant handling HMRC for you? There's a separate income-and-expenses ledger just for that — tidy records you can export and hand straight over.

1:02–1:15 Friday — log your mileage 39 words · ~16s

On screen

Mileage page → Add Trip → journey and miles entered → save → the summary updates with the amount you can claim → convert it to a digital record (same move as Tuesday's receipt) so it feeds the quarterly update.

Voiceover

Friday, driving out to a job. Log the trip — the journey and the miles — and FileThat works out what you can claim. Convert it to a digital record, same as the receipt, and it counts toward your quarter too.

1:15–1:38 The payoff — the quarter fills itself in 53 words · ~23s

On screen

A calendar flicks forward to quarter end → the quarterly update screen with the figures already filled in from the records we just made (read-only) → a slow scroll down the completed figures → end card: FileThat logo + filethat.co.uk.

Voiceover

That's the whole habit. Five minutes with a cup of tea, once a week. And here's the payoff: when the quarter ends, those little sessions have already done the work — your update for HMRC is waiting, ready for you to review and send. Little and often — and your tax return practically files itself.

Fixed claims — please don't paraphrase these

· "Practically files itself" — keep the hedge word. The review-and-submit step is always in frame and in the VO; never promise it files itself without review.

· "FileThat reads the details off it" — do not strengthen to "does your bookkeeping for you" or "no data entry ever".

· "Works out what you can claim" (mileage) — keep as written; no specific rates or £ amounts in the VO.

· No pricing in this video. If an edit wants a price line, lift the exact wording from Video 1 ("keeping your records is free") — nothing new.
