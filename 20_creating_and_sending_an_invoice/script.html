Creating & Sending an Invoice

Target 1:00 VO ≈ 138 words The get-paid video British English

The brief: create an invoice with line items, send it by email, mark it as paid — and show the payoff: the paid invoice lands in your income records, so you never type the same job in twice. The audience is non-tax-savvy; friendly, plain-spoken, no jargon.

0:00–0:10 The hook — finished the job, get paid 22 words · ~10s

On screen

Open real-world: tools going back in the van, a finished kitchen, a handshake at the door. Your creative call. Cut to the FileThat Invoices page, New Invoice button in view.

Voiceover

Finished the job? Let's get you paid. Here's how to send an invoice from FileThat — and what happens when the money lands.

0:10–0:30 Build it — customer and line items 45 words · ~20s

On screen

The create flow (I'll record the product shots):
  • New Invoice → customer details: E Collins
  • Line item added: "Kitchen refit — final payment" · £4,850.00
  • The total updates → a beat on the finished invoice, ready to send

Voiceover

Create a new invoice and add your customer's details. Then add your line items — what you did and what it costs. Here that's the final payment on a kitchen refit: four thousand, eight hundred and fifty pounds. Check the total, and it's ready to go.

Footage note: the on-screen total must read £4,850.00 — it has to match the E Collins payment on the demo bank statement (16 June 2026) and the Q1 income figures. Any tax/VAT lines the invoice form shows stay on screen only; the VO stays out of them to keep this jargon-free.

0:30–0:44 Send it — straight to their inbox 31 words · ~14s

On screen

Press Send → the email preview with the invoice attached → sent confirmation → the invoice list showing its status as Sent — awaiting payment, with any other outstanding invoices visible at a glance.

Voiceover

Now send it — straight from FileThat to your customer's email. No printing, no chasing round with paperwork. You can see at a glance which invoices are still waiting to be paid.

0:44–1:02 Mark as paid — it becomes income 40 words · ~18s

On screen

Status flipped to Paid → cut to the income records: the entry is there — "Kitchen refit — final payment" · £4,850.00 — and the running total ticks up. Hold on that; it's the payoff shot. End card: FileThat logo + filethat.co.uk.

Voiceover

When the money arrives, mark the invoice as paid — and here's the good bit: it goes straight into your income records. No typing it in twice. The job's done, the invoice is paid, and your books already know about it.

Fixed claims — please don't paraphrase these

· "It goes straight into your income records" — the paid invoice becomes an income record; do not extend to "your taxes are done" or "it files itself". If the edit reaches towards HMRC, the most it can say is that digital records feed your submissions.

· Tax/VAT on the invoice appears on screen only — the VO never explains VAT treatment in this video.

· No pricing in this video. If an edit wants a price line, lift the exact wording from Video 1 ("keeping your records is free") — nothing new.
