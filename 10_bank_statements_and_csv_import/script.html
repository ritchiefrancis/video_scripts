Bank Statements & CSV Import

Target 1:00 VO ≈ 146 words The fast-catch-up video British English

The brief: upload a bank statement, watch FileThat sort it into categories, fix the one it gets wrong, and turn the lot into digital records. This is the video for the person who's behind on their books — the message is catch up in minutes, not evenings. The audience is non-tax-savvy; no jargon.

0:00–0:12 The hook — don't type it, upload it 26 words · ~12s

On screen

Open on the pain — a long bank statement on a laptop, someone typing figures into a spreadsheet one row at a time. Your creative call. Cut to the FileThat bank import page with an Upload button front and centre.

Voiceover

Got a pile of bank transactions and no time to type them in? Don't. Download a statement from your bank and let FileThat do the sorting.

0:12–0:34 Upload the CSV — categories appear 50 words · ~22s

On screen

The import flow (I'll record the product shots):
  • Upload demo-bank-transactions.csv → brief processing moment
  • The rows appear with categories already assigned — linger long enough to read a few: Wickes Build Manchester £1,311.74 → Materials, Shell Petrol Station £95.20 → Van & travel, Vodafone Mobile £28.33 → Phone & admin, P Whittaker patio deposit £1,400.00 → Income
  • Caption on the processing moment: "Bank details are removed automatically — FileThat doesn't keep them"

Voiceover

Upload the CSV file and FileThat reads every line for you — sorting each one into a category. Materials from the builders' merchants, fuel, your phone bill, and the money coming in from customers, all recognised automatically. And any bank details in the file are stripped out — FileThat doesn't keep them.

0:34–0:50 Fix one — the coffee isn't business 36 words · ~16s

On screen

Scroll to Costa Coffee Manchester — £4.85 → tap the category → change it to Personal — excluded → the row visibly greys out or drops from the business totals. The Tesco rows and the £500 transfer to savings are nearby, already marked personal — a quick glance at them sells the point without dwelling.

Voiceover

It won't get everything right — nothing does. Your statement has real life in it too. That four pounds eighty-five coffee? Just mark it as personal and it stays out of your business records. One tap, sorted.

Read note: say the price as "four pounds eighty-five", not "four eighty-five".

0:50–1:05 Convert — three months, done 34 words · ~15s

On screen

Press Convert to records → the transactions land as digital records → a slow beat on the tidy records list, running totals visible. End card: FileThat logo + filethat.co.uk.

Voiceover

Happy with the categories? Convert them into digital records in one go. Three months of banking becomes tidy books in a couple of minutes — and it's those records that feed your updates to HMRC.

Footage note: the CSV in this folder (demo-bank-transactions.csv) is the staging asset — 31 transactions, 6 April to 5 July 2026, reconciled to the demo dataset. The personal rows (Tesco, Costa, the savings transfer) are in there deliberately for beat 3; don't tidy them out.

Fixed claims — please don't paraphrase these

· "Any bank details in the file are stripped out — FileThat doesn't keep them" — keep it to this. No broader security promises ("bank-level security", "completely safe", etc.).

· "It won't get everything right — nothing does" — keep the honesty line. Never promise perfect categorisation; the correction moment is the point of beat 3.

· "Those records feed your updates to HMRC" — records feed updates; never "file themselves" or "your taxes are done".

· No pricing in this video. If an edit wants a price line, lift the exact wording from Video 1 ("keeping your records is free") — nothing new.
