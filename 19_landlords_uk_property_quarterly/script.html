Landlords: UK Property Quarterly

Target 1:30 VO ≈ 200 words The landlord quarterly video British English

The brief: the quarterly journey for rental income, mirroring the Video 5/7 rhythm for a landlord audience: log the rent and the costs as they happen → the quarter ends → the update is already filled in → review and submit. The viewer is a landlord, possibly with a day job or a trade as well — non-tax-savvy, no jargon, and the tone is "this is less work than you feared". Demo property: one rental at £950/month on the uk-property business, Q1 2026-27 (6 Apr–5 Jul 2026), update due 7 Aug 2026.

0:00–0:14 The hook — same rhythm, rental edition 31 words · ~14s

On screen

Open on the real world: a "To Let" board, keys handed over, or a bank app showing rent arriving — your creative call. Cut to FileThat with the UK property business selected, so the viewer immediately sees their world (a rental, not a trade) reflected in the app.

Voiceover

If you rent out a property, Making Tax Digital works to the same rhythm as any other business: keep records as you go, then send HMRC a short update each quarter.

0:14–0:30 Log the rent as it lands 35 words · ~16s

On screen

Income & Expenditure ledger for the property → Add Entry → Income → description (e.g. "May rent"), amount £950.00 → save → the running total ticks up. Show a couple of earlier months already in the list, £950 each.

Voiceover

When the rent arrives, log it — a minute's job. This rental is £950 a month, so that's one entry: what it was, how much, done. Do it when it happens and it never piles up.

0:30–0:46 Log the costs too 37 words · ~16s

On screen

Add an expense entry (e.g. a repair receipt snapped on the phone, or a letting-agent invoice) → then a mortgage-interest entry, visibly in its own category. The staged entries sum to the quarter's figures: £640 of expenses plus £890 mortgage interest.

Voiceover

Costs work the same way. A repair, letting fees, insurance — snap the receipt or add the entry as it comes in. Mortgage interest counts too — FileThat keeps it in its own lane, because HMRC treats it differently.

0:46–1:11 The quarter ends — it's already filled in 56 words · ~25s

On screen

Your Filing Deadlines → the open UK property quarter, 6 Apr – 5 Jul 2026, due 7 Aug 2026 → open it → the update form loads pre-filled and read-only: rent £2,850, expenses £640, mortgage interest £890 → hold on the "Pre-filled from digital records" banner, then a slow scroll down the figures.

Voiceover

Then the quarter ends. Your Filing Deadlines shows it waiting — this one covers April to July, due the seventh of August. Open it, and here's the payoff: the figures are already filled in from those records — three months of rent, £2,850, your expenses and mortgage interest, all in the right boxes. You haven't typed a number.

1:11–1:30 Review, submit — and that's the quarter 42 words · ~19s

On screen

Scroll back up → Submit → success state with the HMRC receipt visible under Proof of Submission → cut back to Filing Deadlines showing the quarter now met → end card: FileThat logo + filethat.co.uk.

Voiceover

Give it a once-over — if something looks wrong, fix the record and the form follows. Happy? Press submit, and HMRC gives you a receipt. That's the whole quarter: log things as they happen, then review and submit. Same again in three months.

Footage note: this is the landlord twin of the quarterly-update shot in Videos 5 and 7, staged on the uk-property business rather than the trade — record it in the same session as Video 25's foreign-property version; the two scripts share the same click path.

Fixed claims — please don't paraphrase these

· "You just review it and press submit" is the shape of every filing claim — never say or imply the update files itself without the review step on screen and in the VO.

· "Fix the record and the form follows" — keep as written; do not strengthen to "edit the form" (the form is read-only by design).

· "Mortgage interest counts too — FileThat keeps it in its own lane" — do not turn this into tax advice about how much relief anyone gets; no percentages, no £ savings.

· No pricing in this video, and no specific tax-saving promises anywhere.
