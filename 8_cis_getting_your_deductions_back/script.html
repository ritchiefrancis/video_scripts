CIS: Getting Your Deductions Back

Target 1:30 VO ≈ 191 words The reassurance-for-subbies video British English

The brief: for construction subcontractors who have 20% taken off every payment under CIS. The message: that money isn't lost. FileThat shows the deductions HMRC has on record — contractor by contractor, month by month — and those deductions are set against the year's tax bill. Audience is non-tax-savvy; keep it warm and concrete, no jargon beyond "CIS" itself (which we explain in one line). All figures below are the staged demo dataset — please don't substitute others.

0:00–0:13 The hook — the missing 20% 30 words · ~13s

On screen

Open real-world, not product: a subcontractor's payment statement from a contractor, the deduction line circled or highlighted. Your creative call — a van tailgate, a kitchen table, a folder of statements. Then cut to FileThat.

Voiceover

If you work in construction, you'll know the drill: your contractor takes twenty per cent off before they pay you. That's the Construction Industry Scheme — and that money isn't lost.

0:13–0:31 Your CIS statement — what HMRC has on record 40 words · ~18s

On screen

Self Assessment → CIS Deductions → the current-position screen for 2026–27, slow scroll down the contractor rows:
  • Bardsley Construction Ltd (ref 123/AB45678) — April: £2,400.00 gross · £350.00 materials · £410.00 deducted; May: £1,800.00 · £200.00 · £320.00
  • Millbrook Homes Ltd (ref 456/MH98765) — June: £1,950.00 · £150.00 · £360.00

Voiceover

Open your CIS statement in FileThat and there it all is — every contractor, every month, straight from HMRC's records. Bardsley Construction in April and May, Millbrook Homes in June — what you were paid, the materials, and exactly what was deducted.

0:31–0:52 The totals — tax you've already paid 48 words · ~21s

On screen

Hold on the totals row: £6,150 gross · £700 materials · £1,090 deducted. Then a highlight or gentle zoom on the £1,090 — this number is the whole video.

Voiceover

Add it up and this quarter that's six thousand one hundred and fifty pounds paid, and one thousand and ninety pounds deducted. Here's the bit that matters: that one thousand and ninety isn't gone. It's tax you've already paid — HMRC is holding it with your name on it.

0:52–1:11 The payoff — set against your bill 42 words · ~19s

On screen

Stay on the CIS statement screen. The £1,090 total remains highlighted; an on-screen caption carries the payoff: "Set against your Self Assessment bill — you only pay the difference." A simple motion-graphic arrow from the £1,090 towards a "Your tax bill" label is fine; a product screen of the calculation is not (see note).

Voiceover

When the year's tax is worked out, those deductions are set against the bill — tax already paid, taken off what you owe. So you're not paying twice, and you're not chasing anyone: every pound your contractors deducted counts towards the year's bill.

Fixed claims — please don't paraphrase these

· Deductions are "set against" / "count towards" the year's bill — keep exactly this framing. Never promise a refund, a specific refund amount, or a refund timescale. The word "refund" should not appear in the VO.

· "Straight from HMRC's records" — this screen shows what HMRC holds; don't reword to suggest FileThat calculated or guaranteed these figures.

· "It's tax you've already paid" — keep as written; do not extend to "you'll get it back in January" or any date.

· The £1,090 never appears inside a tax-calculation shot in this video (see the beat 4 production note).

· No pricing in this video.
