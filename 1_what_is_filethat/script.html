What is FileThat?

Target 1:30–2:00 VO ≈ 240 words Public welcome-page video British English

The brief: the high-level pitch for people who have never seen the product — keep digital records → file quarterly to HMRC → pay per filing, no subscription. The audience is non-tax-savvy: friendly, plain-spoken, no jargon. Screen directions below are the lead I'll record; there's deliberate room for your own creative treatment in the opening and closing beats — the voiceover copy and the claims in it are fixed.

0:00–0:17 The hook — why this exists 44 words · ~17s

On screen

Open on something human, not the product — a tradesperson's van, a pile of receipts, a January calendar. Your creative call. End the beat on the FileThat logo or homepage.

Voiceover

If you work for yourself, or you rent out a property, HMRC now wants updates four times a year — not one big return in January. It's called Making Tax Digital, and it sounds like more work. FileThat is built to make it less.

0:17–0:38 Keep simple records 49 words · ~20s

On screen

Quick cuts through the record-keeping surfaces (I'll record these):
  • Snapping a receipt on a phone → it appears as a digital record
  • Adding an income entry (a job payment)
  • Logging a mileage journey
  • Uploading a bank statement → rows auto-categorised

Voiceover

Here's the whole idea. Through the year, you keep simple digital records. Snap a receipt with your phone. Log a job payment. Track your mileage. Or upload a bank statement and let FileThat sort it into categories for you. A few minutes a week is genuinely all it takes.

0:38–1:02 File straight to HMRC 53 words · ~22s

On screen

The quarterly update screen: figures already filled in from the records (read-only), scroll to review, press Submit, land on the HMRC success confirmation. Hold briefly on the confirmation — it's the payoff shot.

Voiceover

Then, once a quarter, FileThat turns those records into your update for HMRC. The figures are already filled in from what you've logged — you just review them and press submit. FileThat connects directly to HMRC, and it's officially recognised for Making Tax Digital, so what you send is exactly what they expect.

1:02–1:24 No subscription — pay per filing 50 words · ~21s

On screen

The pricing message from the homepage: "£3.99 per filing · No subscription" and the comparison line (FreeAgent £228 · Xero £180 · QuickBooks £144 per year). Simple text cards are fine here if you'd rather build a graphic than use the site.

Voiceover

And here's what makes FileThat different: there's no subscription. Keeping your records is free. You only pay when you actually file — three pounds ninety-nine a filing, and you're only charged when the filing succeeds. Most people will spend around twenty pounds a year. The big-name apps charge that every month.

Read note: say the price as "three pounds ninety-nine", not "three ninety-nine".

1:24–1:42 Close — try it free 39 words · ~16s

On screen

Sign-up page, then your end card: FileThat logo + filethat.co.uk. Creative treatment welcome on the end card.

Voiceover

You can try it without spending a penny — sign up free and you'll get four filing credits to start you off. FileThat. Keep your records. File your taxes. Pay per filing, not per month. Find us at filethat.co.uk.

Fixed claims — please don't paraphrase these

· "£3.99 per filing" and "no subscription" — exact wording.

· "Officially recognised for Making Tax Digital" — do not strengthen to "approved partner", "endorsed", etc.

· "Only charged when the filing succeeds" and "four free filing credits on sign-up" — keep as written.

· Competitor prices (£228 / £180 / £144 a year) appear on screen only — the VO stays at "the big-name apps charge that every month".
